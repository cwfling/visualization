> Data source
https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/ttc-streetcar-delay-data/
> What software did you use to create your data visualization?
I used python to create the visualization
> Who is your intended audience? 
The audience could be anyone from the general public, TTC stakeholders, drivers, or engineers trying to figure out which routes are problematic and have the longest delays. From there they could go deeper into what caused the issues and address these issues
> What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 
I am trying to highlight or outline which routes are problematic. Users can see this chart and identify routes that have a large amount of delays. They could then go further into their analysis by looking into what types of delays are happening along the route and what can be done to solve them. 
> What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 
Some of the aspects that I tried to follow are substantive, perceptive, and aesthetic qualities. I made sure that the data is easily readable in both qualitative and quantitative aspects by ensuring things are readable and recognizable. The graph is substantive as there is no scaling to adjust the number of delays occuring. The perception is that having delays of over 30 minutes is considered bad by usage of colour. Although this is subjective, it can be changed by altering the code. I tried to incorporate some of the Gestalt Principles into my visualization. One of these included was enclosure - I did this by including a vertical line to separate a threshold where there was a considerable delay to split the groups in addition to using different colours. 
> How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 
I ensured that the data was reproducible by included the code and setting values that might change dataset to dataset like bin width and x axis tick marks. 
> How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  
I made sure that the graph is accessible by using various means to describe the data. Text was included to describe key components of the plot. In addition to this, I used colours that would be able to be differentiated by different types of colourblindness via selection from colour blindness simulators and recommended palettes.
> Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  
People who use the TTC streetcars as well as the TTC organization
> How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 
When evaluating the data, I was asking myself what I could pull out to tackle issues in the TTC
