> Data source
https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/ttc-streetcar-delay-data/
> What software did you use to create your data visualization?
I used PowerBI to create the visualization
> Who is your intended audience? 
The audience could be anyone from the general public, TTC stakeholders, drivers, or engineers trying to figure out which days of the week or seasons are problematic and have the longest delays. From there they could go deeper into what caused the issues and address these issues. 
> What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 
I am trying to highlight or outline which particular days of the week or seasons are problematic for TTC streetcar run times. Users can look at the current displayed information to see if particular seasons or days have more delays than the rest. They could then go further into their analysis by interacting with the graphs by clicking on the seasons to see their distribution of delays in the days section due to the functionality of PowerBI.
> What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 
Some of the aspects that I tried to follow are substantive and aesthetic qualities. I made sure that the data is easily readable in both qualitative and quantitative aspects by ensuring things are readable and recognizable. The graph is substantive as there is no scaling to adjust the number of delays occuring. I also tried to incorporate dynamic elements by using PowerBI. Users are able to interact with the elements and see how many of the delays belong to a certain season when clicking on it as it is reflected in the changed days of the week graph. 
> How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 
I ensured that the data was reproducible by including some DAX to enable specific filtering of the data like the months corresponding to certain seasons.
> How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  
I made sure that the graph is accessible by using various means to describe the data. Text was included to describe key components of the plot. In addition to this, I used colours that would be able to be differentiated by different types of colourblindness via selection from colour blindness simulators and recommended palettes.
> Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  
People who use the TTC streetcars as well as the TTC organization
> How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 
When evaluating the data, I was asking myself what I could pull out to tackle issues in the TTC. This piece of data could be used in conjunction with more situational data like holidays, road closures, big events, etc to give additional nuance.
